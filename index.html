Know the land.
Recognise its value.

LandBook derives natural-capital value from the services a landscape provides: carbon regulation, water cycling, soil formation and biodiversity support.

Compare future scenarios, identify risks and direct care where it matters. Ecosystem-service value is not the same as cash income.

      The practice

      A collection needs a custodian.
      A living library needs a rhythm.

      Care for the collection
      A named custodian. Clear cataloguing. Specialist care for specimens and seeds.
      Make knowledge traceable
      Keep provenance, dates and methods. Distinguish observation from interpretation.
      Bring people into the work
      Estate teams, local knowledge holders and invited researchers, with agreed access and consent.

      The patron

      The first commission

      Begin with one estate.

      A founding patron helps shape the first library around a real landscape and its people.

      1. Walk & listen

        Meet the custodians. Read the land, its records and the hopes for its future.

      2. Shape the brief

        Choose a site and a purpose. Define the collection, access and design approach.

      3. Agree the care

        Set the budget, permissions, specialist roles and long-term operating plan.

      4. Create & continue

        Build or adapt the space. Gather the first collection. Begin the observation cycle.

      Proposed commissioning path / No estate, budget, building design or delivery date is committed.
